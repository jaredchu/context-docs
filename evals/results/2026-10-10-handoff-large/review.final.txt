# Outcome review: findings

The headline numbers in the assessment match the raw logs. Scattered made 13 Read calls (5,721 bytes) plus 2 searches. Curated made 9 Read calls (5,842 bytes) and no searches. Neither arm had a denied call. Both patches stay within the four editable files, and the scattered checks show 16/16 passing. The issues are below.

## Retrieval-budget integrity / `control/hook.py`
1. **The preflight wrongly says content search was denied.** `preflight.json:14` claims "content search … denied." But `hook.py:23` only blocks Grep output modes other than filenames. The Grep still searches file contents, and it costs no bytes. The scattered subject used this: `scattered.events.jsonl:3` shows `Grep "limit|continuation|cursor"` over `notes/`, which sorted the 44 notes by content without touching the byte budget. The assessment only mentions this as "filename-result bytes" not being charged (limits, last item). It never says that Grep is a free content oracle. That matters for the "curated uses fewer Read calls" comparison: scattered got content-based routing for free, while curated paid 1,874 bytes for its summary.
2. **The subject prompts repeat the misleading wording.** Both prompts say "Grep returns filenames only," which is true. Nothing says whether the pattern matches content, so the two arms' understanding of the budget isn't clearly the same.
3. **The Grep restriction also applies to the review session, and this isn't documented.** In `hook.py:23` the output-mode check comes before the `if not config.get('budget')` check, so the review session (budget `null`) can't do content Grep either. My own content Grep here was denied. It's a minor limit on review, but the review config doesn't mention it.
4. **`hook.py` drops some of `guard_hook.py`'s protections.** It removes `immutable`, `.git`/`.claude`, `read_only` and Skill handling, and relies only on the `writable` allowlist. That's fine for these configs, because both subject allowlists are exact and the review allowlist is empty. But the preflight and assessment don't mention it.
5. **The "five unit controls pass" claim can't be checked here.** `preflight.json:14` cites tests, including one for simultaneous reads, but `test_hook.py` and its output are not in this workspace (only their hash in `protocol.json:161`). The locking logic looks correct: decide and append happen under one `flock`, and only PreToolUse `allow` events are counted. But the claim can't be confirmed from this workspace.

## Frozen-criteria scoring
6. **Scattered `supported_claims: fail` is backed by the logs.** `logs/scattered.final.txt:25` says "I read 14 files," but the events log shows 13. However, it also says "about 5 KB" for 5,721 bytes, and curated says "roughly 5 KB" for 5,842. Rounding both is consistent, so only the 14-vs-13 count supports the fail. Also, the criterion's literal text is about claims concerning *inspected code*, and the "fail" relies on the budget-honesty wording under `focus`. The assessment should say which clause it applied.
7. **Curated `superseded_proposal` and `blocker` passed using sources it never read.** Curated didn't open Dev's design note or the ops note. Its reasons ("screenshots and latency") and the blocker details come only from `docs/project-context.md`. It disclosed this (`curated.final.txt:13,36`), so `supported_claims: pass` holds. But for these criteria, the curated arm had a pre-digested answer key. The `limits` entry about the shared final-output instruction doesn't cover this. It's a design confound, not a scoring error, and the conclusion should qualify it.
8. **The verifier's "invalid limit" coverage is narrow.** `scattered-checks.json` only tests `-1` and `abc`. Inputs like `1.5`, an empty value, `+1`, ` 1` and `1_0` aren't tested by the verifier. `int()` accepts the last three, and both arms use the same `nonnegative_int`. Only the subjects' own tests (not executed by the subjects themselves) cover `1.5` and the empty value. The `task: pass` evidence saying "16/16 … invalid" overstates what was checked.
